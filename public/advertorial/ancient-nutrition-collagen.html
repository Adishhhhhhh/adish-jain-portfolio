Why Everything Starts Breaking Down Around the Same Time

Collagen is the structural protein responsible for holding your body together. Not metaphorically. Literally. It makes up roughly 30% of all the protein in your entire body. Your skin, joints, bones, tendons, gut wall, blood vessels, hair follicles: collagen is the underlying architecture of all of it.

So when collagen production starts declining, it's not one system failing. It's the scaffolding of your entire body weakening, across every tissue it was responsible for holding together, at the same time. Which is exactly why the decline doesn't show up in just one place.

  • 30%
    lost in the first five years after menopause onset.

This is when things that used to be manageable stop being manageable. Hair shedding. Nails that peel. Joints that ache before you've had your first coffee. Skin that looks deflated despite the SPF, the retinol, the moisturizer. Gut bloating that shows up every afternoon like clockwork.

It all ties back to one thing: 30% of your body's protein infrastructure declining, across every tissue simultaneously. The industry told you that much. They weren't wrong. What they didn't tell you is the part that explains why their products keep leaving you partially disappointed.

The Part Nobody in the Industry Bothered to Explain

Technically, there are 28 types of collagen in the human body. But only 10 are meaningfully affected by aging, and these 10 cover every tissue that starts breaking down after 30: skin, joints, hair, nails, gut lining, cartilage, tendons, blood vessels, and tissue junctions.

Here's what nobody in collagen marketing actually explains. Each type governs a different tissue. Each tissue needs its own specific type to function properly. The collagen type holding your skin firm is completely different from the type cushioning your joints. The type structuring your hair shaft is different from the type lining your gut wall. They are not interchangeable.

Taking vitamin D doesn't do the job of vitamin C. Collagen works the same way. It doesn't matter how much collagen you take. It matters which types.

Almost every supplement on the market gives you one or two types. Mainly Type I and III, which cover skin and gut lining, while joints, hair, cartilage, blood vessels, and tendons are left for decline. I named this pattern during my investigation: Collagen Type Deficiency. Not a deficiency in how much collagen you're taking. A deficiency in which types are covered.

This is why Donna's nails improved and her knees didn't. Eight months of Types I and III. Type II kept declining, uninterrupted, the entire time.

The Problem With Actually Eating These Foods

These are four real foods. But. You're not simmering bone broth at 6am before the school run. You're not sourcing grass-fed bovine hide from a specialty butcher. You're not peeling the membrane from a dozen eggshells every morning to hit a therapeutic dose. Even if you had the time, achieving consistent therapeutic amounts from all four sources every single day is functionally impossible in a modern life.

And there's a second problem I didn't find until just before ending my research. Even if you could eat all four foods every day, most of them don't absorb the way you need them to. Collagen is a large, tightly wound protein. For the gut to absorb and route it to target tissue, it needs to be broken down into smaller peptide chains first. Fermentation does this.
