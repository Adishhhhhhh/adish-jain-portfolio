Why Everything Starts Breaking Down Around the Same Time

Here's the context that makes everything else make sense.

Collagen is the structural protein responsible for holding your body together. Not metaphorically. Literally.

It makes up roughly 30% of all the protein in your entire body. Your skin, joints, bones, tendons, gut wall, blood vessels, hair follicles: collagen is the underlying architecture of all of it.

So when collagen production starts declining, it's not one system failing. It's the scaffolding of your entire body weakening, across every tissue it was responsible for holding together, at the same time.

Which is exactly why the decline doesn't show up in just one place.

After 30, your body produces 1% less collagen every single year. By 40, roughly 10% of your structural collagen is gone. By the time you enter menopause, the rate accelerates dramatically, up to 30% lost in the first five years after onset.

This is when things that used to be manageable stop being manageable.

Hair shedding. Nails that peel. Joints that ache before you've had your first coffee. Skin that looks deflated despite the SPF, the retinol, the moisturizer. Gut bloating that shows up every afternoon like clockwork.

It all ties back to one thing: 30% of your body's protein infrastructure declining, across every tissue simultaneously.

The industry told you that much. They weren't wrong.

What they didn't tell you is the part that actually explains why their products keep leaving you partially disappointed.

The Part Nobody in the Industry Bothered to Explain

Technically, there are 28 types of collagen in the human body. But only 10 are meaningfully affected by aging, and these 10 cover every tissue that starts breaking down after 30: skin, joints, hair, nails, gut lining, cartilage, tendons, blood vessels, and tissue junctions.

Here's what nobody in collagen marketing actually explains.

Each type governs a different tissue. Each tissue needs its own specific type to function properly.

The collagen type holding your skin firm is completely different from the type cushioning your joints. The type structuring your hair shaft is different from the type lining your gut wall.

They are not interchangeable.

Think of it like vitamins. Taking vitamin D doesn't do the job of vitamin C. They work on completely different systems.

Collagen works the same way. Each type has a specific role, a specific tissue, a specific job.

It doesn't matter how much collagen you take. It matters which types.

Almost every supplement on the market gives you one or two types. Mainly Type I and III, which cover skin and gut lining. While joints, hair, cartilage, blood vessels, and tendons are left for decline.
